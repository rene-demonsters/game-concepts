# Make Yourself at Home (working title)

A surreal melee action thriller about people who love you, incompatible versions of your life, and the wonderful things they ask you to do to other people.

You explore welcoming places, take on assignments for people you trust, and commit to spectacular violence before you can establish whose story is true. Each job brings something home: a person, an object, a favour, a consequence. Your household remembers a life you increasingly cannot reconcile with your own.

**Player promise:** satisfying, excessive combat inside a world that makes you doubt your interpretation of events. Your actions matter even when their meaning changes.

This is a standalone concept informed by Mascot Mayhem, rather than a proposed rewrite of that game. The title, setting details, cast names, and final explanation remain provisional. Title availability has not been checked.

## Premise

You return to a cheerful shared house after an absence nobody explains the same way twice. Your favourite meal is waiting. Someone has kept your room exactly as you left it. Someone else apologises for what they did to it. Both descriptions appear to be correct.

Your housemates are affectionate, funny, useful, and individually convincing. One knows how you take your tea. Another knows what you said during a fight nobody survived. A third has photographs of a holiday you remember spending with somebody else.

They help you get back on your feet by finding work around town. Retrieve a missing performer. Settle a dispute at an apartment building. Bring a birthday present home. The requests sound personal and manageable. The places are inviting. Your ability to turn them into colourful disasters is extraordinary.

You are an enthusiastic participant. You enjoy the fighting, appreciate being wanted, and have reasons to accept the rewards. Discovering manipulation does not automatically make you repentant. You may decide that somebody who has lied about your entire life still makes an excellent breakfast.

The central question is: **why does everyone need you to keep enjoying this?**

## Design pillars

- **Pleasure with consequences.** Combat is exuberant and the protagonist can be knowingly awful. People remember what you destroy, preserve, and bring home.
- **Warmth worth returning to.** Home contains affection, jokes, routines, arguments, and useful relationships. It must be appealing before its contradictions become threatening.
- **Uncertainty that affects action.** Conflicting accounts change whom you help, which route you take, what you defend, and how encounters unfold.
- **Dependable combat inside impossible events.** Attacks, dodges, enemy tells, and learned interactions stay readable. Reality can fold without making the controls dishonest.
- **Authored contradictions.** Repetition lets players recognise specific changes. Discoveries support theories and predictions; strange events are placed for a reason.

## What kind of game it is

A single-player action adventure with a top-down or elevated three-quarter camera. Compact, inhabited locations combine exploration, conversation, preparation, and fast melee combat in the same space.

Borrow Hades's responsiveness, expressive attack combinations, and developing relationships. Borrow Hitman's attention to places, routines, and preparation that changes an encounter. Keep the scope focused on short authored assignments rather than a large simulation or an extensive roguelike progression system.

The initial pacing target is roughly 10–15 minutes per assignment, with quieter household scenes between them. This is a prototype hypothesis, not a fixed timer. Exploring should create anticipation without becoming a compulsory search for every clue.

Combat starts through an action in the world: taking an object, opening a door, escorting somebody out, sabotaging a device, or attacking. The room where you chatted becomes the room you wreck.

## Core loop

1. **Come home.** Reconnect with the household, see the consequences of the previous job, choose equipment, and pick up a request. Someone offers a plausible explanation for your circumstances.
2. **Get acquainted.** Enter a small location, meet its residents, observe routines, and investigate opportunities. Establish a practical objective even if the motive is disputed.
3. **Prepare.** Secure an advantage through exploration or a relationship: an escape route, an ally, control of a hazard, or knowledge of an enemy weakness.
4. **Commit through action.** Decide what to carry, protect, destroy, release, or leave behind. You can act with incomplete information, or start violence immediately and accept the consequences.
5. **Fight through the result.** Use expressive melee combat and the environment to complete your chosen objective. Impossible events can challenge your understanding while preserving readable combat rules.
6. **Bring the outcome back.** An object changes the house, a rescued person moves in, a favour becomes an obligation, or a relationship deteriorates. A new contradiction changes how you interpret the job.

## Decisions and investigation

The recurring design question is **whose claim will you physically protect?** It is a writing tool, not a menu that appears on every mission.

One assignment might present incompatible requests. Another has everyone agreeing on the facts but disagreeing about what to do. A third reveals a clear truth whose consequences are harder to accept than the original uncertainty. Some jobs allow compromise or refusal; others impose a genuine conflict. The outcome follows the available situation, rather than a universal binary trust system.

Preparation must improve your ability to act. A photograph can reveal a service entrance. A conversation can establish that an apparently friendly creature attacks when music stops. Helping a resident can earn support during the fight. Lore is welcome, but it cannot carry the entire exploration phase.

Knowledge remains valuable when interpretation fails. You can be wrong about who recorded a song and still correctly exploit its effect on an enemy.

An optional journal records observed events and attributed claims separately: “I brought the recording home”; “Mara says it belonged to my sister.” It preserves useful details without grading theories or declaring a final account of reality. Confusion should concern the story, not remembering the objective or operating the interface.

## Combat and progression

Start with light attack, heavy attack, dodge, and contextual interaction. Build expression from timing, combinations, positioning, and using objects around you. Hits need weight: anticipation, impact pauses, strong sound, knockback, and readable recovery.

Enemies and scenery burst into candy gore: frosting, jam, sprinkles, stuffing, and other excessive materials. The violence is celebratory enough to sustain the protagonist's appetite for it. Quiet aftermaths and personal reactions can make the same spectacle feel different later, without requiring every fight to end in a moral lecture.

Preparation changes the physical problem. Defending an object rewards holding ground; carrying it changes mobility; cutting a compressor disables moving inflatable barriers. Shared rooms and enemies can support different objectives without requiring separate campaigns.

Weapons and a small number of equipment modifiers support different approaches. Relationships can grant tools, access, or assistance, so accepting help also deepens dependence. Permanent progression expands options rather than requiring repetitive grinding for enough damage to continue.

Use authored campaign progression and encounter checkpoints initially. Death retries the current encounter. Story consequences arise from completed actions, abandonment, and authored objective failures. Replaying after death does not silently select a different history. Stranger death rules are a later possibility only if they strengthen the core experience.

## Home and the recurring cast

The house gradually becomes a physical record of your decisions. A preserved recording plays in the kitchen. A rescued person occupies the spare room. A damaged gift remains damaged even as people disagree about who gave it to you.

Begin with three housemates, each with a practical role, an affectionate routine, and an account of your absence:

| Role | What makes them valuable | The pressure they create |
|---|---|---|
| The organiser | Finds work, supplies equipment, remembers your preferences | Treats your return as recovery and steers you toward familiar behaviour |
| The confidant | Offers companionship, local introductions, and intimate knowledge | Says the others have mistaken you for somebody you once pretended to be |
| The dependent | Maintains the house and helps with things you bring back | Seems to need your assignments to continue, and fears what remembering might change |

These are provisional dramatic roles. None is designated the reliable exposition character or inevitable traitor. Each needs desires beyond managing the protagonist, and each must sometimes be straightforwardly right.

Relationships develop through what you do and accept, rather than a visible universal trust score. A person can love you, deceive you, and provide reliable help in the same scene.

## Rules for unreliable reality

The player should lose confidence in their explanation of events while retaining reasons to investigate and act.

- Never invent a consequential player decision and insist they made it. An NPC can falsely accuse the protagonist; the game must distinguish that accusation from rewriting the player's input.
- Preserve the consequences of completed actions. Bringing someone home remains part of the story, even if their identity becomes disputed.
- Physical impossibilities are allowed: looping corridors, impossible arrivals, changed recordings. They must follow authored patterns and preserve the practical consequences on which choices relied.
- Earned preparation advantages survive a shift. If a song disables a particular enemy, its effect stays dependable through that encounter even when its apparent source changes.
- Combat hazards retain tells and response time. A narrative surprise does not justify unavoidable damage or an unannounced control change.
- Resolve local mysteries regularly. Players should discover real answers and make successful predictions while the larger account remains unsettled.
- Allow ordinary moments and stable relationships. If every friendly gesture signals a betrayal, the player stops engaging with people.

The writing team needs a private causal account of the central contradictions before expanding the campaign. The audience can remain uncertain without the authors improvising incompatible explanations.

## Example assignment: the birthday recording

You arrive at a pastel indoor play centre to retrieve a present for someone at home. The rabbit entertainer recognises you. The guests appear happy. Several thank you for arranging the party.

The rabbit asks you to preserve a recording supposedly containing your missing friend's voice. A parent asks you to destroy it, claiming its song keeps the guests trapped. Both provide credible evidence; neither account explains everything.

Investigation reveals a service route and a testable interaction: playing the recording opens its exit and disables the security dancers, but activates a second set of animatronics. You can prepare around those effects.

You might defend the recording on its pedestal while guests leave, or carry it toward the disposal point and fight with reduced mobility. The same connected rooms support both approaches. Taking control of the recording begins the confrontation; there is no separate “choose whom to believe” screen.

During combat, a corridor returns you to a room you just left. The recording begins speaking in your voice. The security dancers still respond to the song exactly as you learned.

The immediate outcome is legible: who escaped, what you destroyed, whether you kept the recording, and whom you fought. Later, a housemate recognises the voice as yours from childhood. Another insists the party is tomorrow.

On a later visit, damage from your fight remains. A resident sincerely remembers helping you cause it for a different reason. New evidence should support or challenge an existing theory, rather than merely add another unexplained oddity.

## Example assignment: a room of your own

**Request:** a housemate asks you to collect their brother from a cheerful apartment building. They have made up the spare bed. The building's residents insist the brother is already home: he is the building.

The atrium is all hanging plants, floral carpets, warm lamps, and neighbours who know your tea order. A man in a mustard sweater sits beside the brass intercom, connected to it by cloth-covered speaker leads. He asks you to take him outside. Each time he speaks, another apartment answers in his voice. The concierge offers a tenancy agreement instead of an explanation.

**Investigation and preparation:** learn which intercom chimes open each landing. The man can redirect lifts and temporarily hold security shutters; testing those effects makes him useful before you decide what he is. A resident will show you a stairwell that remains connected when the lift stops. Another lends you an absurdly large brass maintenance key: it opens service locks and works as a heavy melee weapon.

**Commitment:** disconnecting the man begins the extraction. It disables the building's automated defenses but also stops its lift, leaving you to escort him through the route you prepared. You can instead help him move to the portable lobby speaker and defend it while he opens the front door himself. That preserves remote assistance but requires holding a position. Leaving without him remains possible; the spare room stays empty and your housemate responds to that decision.

**Combat:** smiling concierge automatons made of upholstered cushion blocks attempt to restore the building's “occupancy.” Velvet ropes divide the floor into lanes, bellhop trolleys become charging hazards, and smashed cushions scatter stuffing and cranberry-jam filling. A concierge pauses to ask whether the damage should come out of your deposit. Heavy key attacks can pin rope posts against walls and open movement space.

**Reality shift:** familiar breakfast rooms repeat on different floors. A stairway delivers you to the landing above the one you climbed toward. The chime patterns and prepared service stairwell remain dependable; the escort's position and the space available for dodging stay readable. The man reacts to damage to the building with specific memories rather than physical wounds: “That was where we had our first argument.”

**What comes home:** if you extract him, he occupies the spare room and the apartment building remains without its intercom voice. At dinner, one housemate introduces him as their brother; another sets a place beside you and addresses him as your husband. He remembers the route you took and offers an explanation for your relationship that the player can investigate. Helping him inhabit the speaker instead brings that object home, with a different practical dependence and relationship outcome. Damage to the atrium persists on return visits in every case.

This assignment tests a rescue whose practical outcome is clear while the rescued person's identity—and the meaning of “home”—remains contested.

## Example assignment: the last train home

**Request:** collect a mustard-yellow suitcase from the station before the last night train leaves. Your housemate says it contains things you left behind during your absence. The station staff recognise the suitcase and agree it is yours. The immediate uncertainty concerns where this journey leads, rather than choosing between two rival mission-givers.

The platform has flower baskets, warm brass lanterns, and a little tea counter. The cream-and-teal carriages are lit like homes. Through one window you see your kitchen. Through another, your bedroom. A housemate reaches through an open carriage door from the breakfast nook and asks why you are taking so long. They were at the house when you left.

**Investigation and preparation:** a departure bell makes the padded porters stop to check their watches for a brief, predictable interval. A manual signal lever keeps the carriage doors open. Inspecting the luggage route reveals an accessible brake and lets you retrieve the suitcase without first crossing the entire platform. A closed umbrella works as a fast parrying weapon and can hook luggage trolleys into attacking porters.

**Commitment:** taking your suitcase marks the end of collection and calls the swan conductor to inspect the platform. You can secure the case and leave on foot, or help the waiting passengers board and release the signal for departure. The passenger you assist can later help reach the rear brake, giving you time to disembark. Boarding with the case places you inside the moving carriages and makes getting off a combat objective, rather than selecting an ending from a dialogue menu. The departure sequence starts with this commitment; quiet investigation is not secretly timed.

**Combat:** a huge padded swan in a plum conductor's uniform sweeps the platform with its wings while porters shove luggage trolleys through narrow gaps. Foam and redcurrant-jam filling burst from damaged uniforms. Adult passengers calmly debate the refund policy while you parry with an umbrella and fight one-handed around the suitcase. Clear bell cues continue to interrupt porters after the setting changes.

**Reality shift:** carriage windows show the same domestic rooms at different times of day. The station clock runs backward, but door signals and departure cues keep their established timing. The person in the kitchen is visibly packing the belongings you are carrying. The platform bench bears the same carved repair as your dining chair.

**What comes home:** an early return preserves the household as you left it, with the suitcase and the consequences of abandoning the passengers. If you see the train depart, the housemate who boarded is absent from home. You witnessed that departure; the uncertainty lies in whether they escaped, left you, or went where they had always belonged. Their room remains accessible and contains evidence of both travel preparations and a life at home. A ticket in the recovered case names tomorrow's date; the clothes inside fit you, but carry someone else's carefully sewn name labels. Helping a passenger establishes a continuing contact through later station visits.

This assignment tests a concrete departure with an emotionally costly outcome. The train changes a relationship the player values, rather than erasing a completed action or revealing that the journey never happened.

## Story direction

**Opening:** establish the pleasure of belonging. Small contradictions are initially funny or easy to dismiss. The first assignment delivers a complete action sequence and one memorable inconsistency.

**Development:** jobs bring competing accounts into the house. The people who make you feel understood increasingly ask for incompatible actions. Evidence links your absence to places you are visiting and people you have harmed. Some apparent lies turn out to have been attempts at protection; affection does not establish innocence.

**Escalation:** the protagonist discovers that assignments help preserve particular versions of shared events. People are recruiting their violence to make a preferred life continue. Their own enthusiasm may be one of the forces sustaining it.

**Ending direction:** make a consequential commitment about whom and what to preserve, with enough established rules to understand the cost. Different commitments can produce different household outcomes without requiring unrelated final acts. The player need not solve every contradiction or become a better person to reach a satisfying conclusion.

A candidate underlying explanation is that incompatible versions of events coexist, sustained by witnesses, objects, and repeated rituals. The household once cooperated to protect the protagonist but now disagrees about which life should survive. This supplies motives and repeatable rules; it is a hypothesis to test during writing, not a locked cosmology or a reality-selection power presented to the player at the outset.

Subjective reality is the creative subject. A psychiatric diagnosis is not the explanation for enjoying violence, and “it was all a dream” would discard the consequences on which the game depends.

## Tone, visuals, and sound

Keep cozy colours and inviting names throughout. Candy gore need not turn into realistic blood for the story to become unsettling. A plush body that keeps breathing or frosting that returns after cleaning can make the established materials disturbing on their own terms.

Humour is excessive, personal, and sometimes appalling. An enemy can charge a cleaning fee halfway through a fight. A housemate can be more offended by your damaged shoes than by what happened to their owner. Characters need distinct attitudes; relentless ironic commentary would flatten them.

Music supports pleasure and contradiction. Warm melodies can grow into aggressive combat arrangements. A household tune can recur in an impossible location. A victory jingle might begin before an apparent confrontation, unsettling the player's expectations while leaving the decision to act intact.

Avoid using generic glitch effects as the main language of uncertainty. Stage changes through blocking, repeated dialogue, props, sound, and remembered spaces. Not every joke conceals trauma, and not every pleasant moment needs a horror sting.

## Existing games and the space for this concept

Research found substantial precedents for the ingredients. The proposed distinction is their interaction: **investigate welcoming places, act on uncertain accounts, and carry lasting consequences into relationships that challenge your understanding.** This is a design direction, not a claim of unprecedented originality.

| Reference | Relevant overlap | Design implication |
|---|---|---|
| [Katana ZERO](https://www.devolverdigital.com/games/katana-zero) | Stylish lethal assignments, time manipulation, and uncovering the protagonist's past | Exploration and relationships must change missions, beyond providing mysterious interludes |
| [killer7](https://store.steampowered.com/app/868520/killer7/) | Surreal assassins, identities, puzzles, and conspiracy | Cryptic assassin fiction alone is not a distinctive premise |
| [No More Heroes](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1420290/) | Excessive melee assassinations, eccentric opponents, and mundane life between battles | Domestic absurdity and spectacular violence are compatible; home must also hold consequences |
| [We Happy Few](https://compulsiongames.com/games/we-happy-few/?lang=en) | Cheerful denial and disturbing realities | Avoid relying on a happy surface concealing one ugly “real world” |
| [Deathloop](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/07/08/deathloop-first-look-at-extended-gameplay/) | Revisiting locations and using knowledge to change approaches | Repeated spaces should reward learning while complicating personal interpretation |
| [Slay the Princess](https://blacktabbygames.com/press-stp) | Instructions to kill, contested accounts, and choices and perceptions shaping the world | Belief must affect action and outcomes; real-time combat provides a different form |
| [Happy's Humble Burger Farm](https://www.scythedev.com/games/burger-farm) | Mascot branding, everyday work, and an increasingly wrong environment | If mascots remain prominent, establish exuberant action early so the promise is clear |

Hotline Miami informs the relationship between pleasurable violence, instructions, and complicity. Legion informs the ambition for persuasive subjective reality. Saints Row informs excessive humour. Hades informs combat feel and relationships that develop between excursions. Mascot Mayhem contributes willing complicity, cozy presentation, and candy gore; its corporate setting and mascot cast are not requirements for this game.

## First prototype

**Play it:** [`prototype/`](prototype/README.md) is a browser-playable version of the birthday assignment, with placeholder art and no dependencies. Run `node serve.mjs` in that folder and open the URL it prints (normally http://localhost:8081/prototype/), or open `prototype/index.html` directly. A run takes 5–10 minutes.

Build one location and a small home scene, then revisit that location after the consequences have reached home.

Include four connected rooms, two people with incompatible requests, one recording, three enemy behaviours, and one melee moveset. Give preparation one visible combat payoff. Support preserving or destroying the recording through shared spaces and encounters. Carry its final state home. On the return visit, preserve physical damage and introduce one specific contradiction with evidence for competing explanations.

Use placeholder art with clear combat feedback and deliberate sound. Defer procedural generation, extensive builds, multiple protagonists, a large town, voice acting, and a broad relationship simulation.

Playtest questions:

- Can players describe what they chose and what happened as a result?
- Do they use investigation to gain an advantage during combat?
- Do they form different explanations supported by details they actually observed?
- Does the contradiction make them reconsider a prior interpretation or seek evidence?
- Do they want to return home and talk to somebody there?
- Do they enjoy fighting enough that accepting another assignment feels tempting?

If players only report confusion, the narrative structure has failed. If they understand the mystery but find exploration a delay before the enjoyable part, preparation has failed. Expand only after the prototype makes both activities worth doing.

## Decisions still open

- Final title, location, and whether the cast are people, mascots, or a mixture.
- The exact cause and limits of reality shifts, recorded privately before writing the full campaign.
- Whether the protagonist speaks freely, uses selected responses, or is defined mostly through action.
- How often refusal, compromise, and leaving an assignment unfinished are supported.
- Camera, platform, campaign length, and production scope, informed by the first prototype.

The current direction is an authored melee action adventure with revisited locations, a recurring household, and consequences that survive uncertainty. Preserve those foundations while testing the setting and explanation.
