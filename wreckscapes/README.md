# Wreckscapes (working title)

A bubble shooter with an upside-down home-renovation meta. Your punk band has moved into an aristocrat's empty mansion. You blast through puzzle levels to earn stars, then spend them **wrecking** the place, room by room, beautifully.

It plays like the cozy "solve puzzles to decorate a house" games people play for years. Instead of making it pretty, you make it yours.

**Player promise:** the relaxing aim-and-pop loop, plus the deep satisfaction of trashing something stuffy and turning it into the best squat in the world.

Title availability has not been checked.

## Pillars

- **Cozy underneath.** Short levels, a gentle difficulty curve, no timers by default. The renovation meta is the reason to keep playing, the same as in the genre's big hits.
- **Destruction is creative.** Wrecking a room is never just breaking things. Each wrecked room becomes something better: the library turns into a skate bowl, the dining room into a stage. Before and after should both look great.
- **Rich stuffiness is the target.** Heirlooms, portraits, porcelain and tradition are the enemies. No people get hurt; things do.
- **The store is part of the joke.** Lives, boosters and offers are in-world items with their own gags.

## Core puzzle: the bubble shooter

### The basics

The board is a cluster of **heirlooms** hanging from the ceiling: teacups, vases, figurines, decorative plates, little framed portraits, all in five or six types. At the bottom, the player's band member fires **junk** at the cluster: bricks, beer bottles, spray cans, guitar picks. The thrown item takes on a type to match.

- Hit 3 or more of the same heirloom type and they shatter.
- Anything left hanging with nothing holding it up falls and smashes, worth extra points.
- Bounce shots off the walls to reach tricky spots.
- The aiming line shows the first bounce. Boosters show more.

### Specials

- **Spray Can:** paints every heirloom it touches the same type.
- **Brick:** goes straight through a line of heirlooms.
- **Cherry Bomb:** blows up a cluster where it lands.
- **Bass Drop:** the band plays one massive note and the whole cluster shakes down one row.
- **Skateboard:** grinds along the edge of the cluster, clearing everything it touches.

### Obstacles

- **Glass cases:** need two hits.
- **Chandeliers:** heavy pieces that take a whole cluster with them when they fall.
- **Family portraits:** eyes follow you. They shuffle nearby heirloom types every few shots.
- **Cobwebs:** sticky cells that catch your shot.
- **The butler's tray:** carries one item and moves along a rail.

### Level goals

- Clear X heirlooms of a given type.
- **Drop the chandelier:** bring down specific chandeliers.
- **Free the band's gear:** guitars and amps are locked inside the cluster; knock them loose.
- Clear the top row (classic "reach the ceiling").

## Meta: the mansion

Each level earns **stars**. Stars are spent on **wrecking tasks** in the current room. Each task shows a small, fun animation and changes the room for good:

- Graffiti over the ancestral portraits.
- Kick the grand piano out of the window.
- Turn the library into a half-pipe.
- Fill the ballroom fountain with foam.
- Put a drum kit in the chapel.

When a room is done, it becomes a usable space for the band (practice room, skate bowl, recording studio, gig stage). Some wrecking tasks give the player a choice of 2-3 styles (pink graffiti or green, a disco ball or a giant flaming skull), so every mansion ends up looking slightly different.

### Rooms (first season)

1. Grand Entrance Hall
2. Dining Room → Stage
3. Library → Skate Bowl
4. Ballroom → Foam Party Pit
5. Wine Cellar → Recording Studio
6. Conservatory → Mutant Plant Jungle
7. Master Bedroom → Band Dorm
8. Gardens → Festival Grounds

Further seasons: the family's ski chalet, their yacht, their private island.

## Story and cast

A funny, warm story told through short cutscenes between wrecking tasks.

- **The band:** four punks, each with a clear personality. **Vex** (singer, chaos leader), **Hammer** (drummer, gentle giant), **Lil' Static** (guitarist, the brains), **Moth** (bassist, barely speaks, best ideas).
- **Fenwick the butler:** the heart of the story. Horrified in room one. Grudgingly helpful by room three. By room six, he's in the band, playing the triangle in a leather vest.
- **Lady Ashcombe-Pryce:** the absent owner who calls with outraged phone messages. Every message sounds worse than the last, and she eventually shows up for the season finale.
- **Neighbours, a local news crew and a society magazine** show up as recurring gags.

## Monetisation

The standard free-to-play renovation-puzzle model, played straight but themed.

- **Lives = guitar strings.** Six strings, one snaps per failed level. They're restrung over time (about 30 minutes each). When all six are gone, wait for the roadie or buy a **new set of strings**.
- **Asking friends:** friends toss you spare strings.
- **Continue screen:** "Out of junk? Raid the attic for 5 more throws."
- **Pre-level boosters:** Spray Can, Cherry Bomb, Long Aim (shows the full bounce path).
- **Unlimited lives:** **"Groupie Pass: 2 hours"**.
- **Season pass:** **"Eviction Notice Pass"**, with free and premium tracks of decor styles and boosters.
- **Rewarded ads:** played on the mansion's old cinema projector.

## Art direction

- **Before:** high-society Victorian/Georgian interiors. Rich and detailed, but slightly muted and dusty, like a stately-home brochure.
- **After:** the band's version is loud, colourful and handmade: neon spray paint, string lights, stickers, posters, DIY ramps. The contrast is the visual payoff.
- Heirlooms are tiny, ornate and fun to break: shatter animations with satisfying shards and puffs of dust.
- Characters are stylised and expressive, with exaggerated proportions and huge hair.

## Audio

- Puzzle levels use relaxed garage-punk and surf-rock instrumentals.
- Every pop is a ceramic shatter, pitched up as cascades grow.
- Rooms get their own music when they're finished, so walking through the house plays the band's songs.
- Fenwick's horrified gasps and Lady Ashcombe-Pryce's voice messages are recurring audio gags.

## Tone and ratings

- Aim for 7+/PEGI 7. This is the gentlest of the punk-cozy concepts: only objects get destroyed, never people.
- The satire is aimed at stuffiness and inherited wealth in a light, cartoon way. No real families, brands or places.
- No alcohol branding. Bottles are generic and empty.

## Open questions

- Is the band "squatting" or did they inherit the house through some ridiculous loophole? The loophole might be funnier and avoids any legal-issue framing.
- Should the player decorate freely (place items anywhere) or only pick from 2-3 preset styles? Presets are cheaper to make and are the genre norm.
- Could Lady Ashcombe-Pryce work as a recurring "boss level" with special mechanics?
- Bubble shooter or match-3 for the core? Bubble shooter fits "throwing things at heirlooms" best and avoids overlap with [Mosh Pit](../mosh-pit/README.md).
