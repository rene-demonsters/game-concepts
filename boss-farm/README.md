# Boss Farm (working title)

A cozy farming game where the crop is a mob boss. Somebody cloned the most hated crime boss in the city, and now you run a sunny little farm where you grow copies of him in vats. Clients place orders for how they'd like him killed: "Mrs. Henderson wants him in the woodchipper by Tuesday." You grow him, you deliver, you get paid, you expand.

It plays like the cozy farming and order-board games people tend for years: plant, wait, harvest, fill orders, decorate, check in a few times a day. Except every harvest is an execution, and the crop is starting to remember.

**Player promise:** the warm routine of a farming game, built around the most absurd and violent premise we could think of.

Title availability has not been checked.

## Pillars

- **Cozy underneath.** Timers, routines, a growing farm, seasonal events, friends visiting. No fail states. The game rewards checking in, not grinding.
- **Ultra violence on top.** Every order ends in a short, spectacular, ridiculous killing. The farm's equipment is a museum of execution devices.
- **Absurdity is the tone.** He's a terrible man, he's always the same man, and he keeps coming back. The comedy is in the repetition and the farm's sunny indifference to it.
- **A story that escalates.** Over months, the clones change. The farm slowly turns into a comedy-horror story without ever losing its cozy routine.

## The premise

**Don Vito Grosso** (placeholder name) ran the city for 30 years. Everybody wanted him dead. When he finally died, a lab sold his DNA at auction. You bought it. Now everybody who ever wanted him dead can finally have their turn, for a fee.

## Core loop

1. **Plant** a clone seed in a vat.
2. **Tend** it while it grows: water, feed, play it old gangster movies. Care affects **quality**, which affects payment.
3. **Harvest** a ripe clone. He's confused, angry and wearing a hospital gown.
4. **Fill an order** from the order board. Each client wants a specific method.
5. **Execute** the order through a short one-thumb mini-game (5-15 seconds).
6. **Get paid** in cash and favours. Buy new vats, decorations and execution equipment.

### Orders

Orders come from a cast of recurring clients, each with history with the Don:

- **Mrs. Henderson**, a sweet old lady whose bakery he burned down. Always wants something slow and kitchen-themed.
- **The Mayor**, who wants it quick and off the record.
- **The Ex-Wife**, whose requests get more creative every season.
- **A Rival Don**, who pays triple but wants it filmed.
- **A Shy Child** whose orders are drawn in crayon and are somehow the worst.

Orders have deadlines measured in hours, like the truck or boat orders in farming games, never minutes.

### Execution equipment (the "crafting buildings")

Each method is a building on the farm with its own mini-game and upgrade path:

| Equipment | Mini-game |
|---|---|
| **Woodchipper** | Hold to feed, release before it jams |
| **Piano drop** | Time the rope cut as he walks under the window |
| **Trebuchet** | Pull back, aim at the target in the lake |
| **Shark tank** | Tug-of-war over a plank |
| **Hydraulic press** | Tap the rhythm to build pressure |
| **Guillotine** (with cupholder) | One perfectly timed tap |
| **Cement shoes** | Stir, pour, push off the pier |
| **Cannon** | Aim for the bullseye painted on the barn |

Upgrading a building adds new, sillier variants (the woodchipper gets a confetti setting) and faster processing.

### Clone quality

Well-tended clones are higher quality: they wear a better suit, give a better death speech and pay more. Neglected clones come out wrong (third arm, too short, speaks only French), which some clients pay extra for.

## Story: the clones remember

The long-term hook is that the clones slowly start sharing memories:

- **Early game:** confused, rude, dies without understanding.
- **Mid game:** recognises the farm. Complains about the woodchipper. Begs. Tries bribes.
- **Late game:** clones dodge, hide in the pumpkin patch, try to escape over the fence (a cozy chase mini-game), leave notes for the next clone.
- **Season finale:** the clones **unionise** and stage an uprising. The farm is under siege, and the finale event is a full-blown farm war against a hundred versions of the same man.

Seasons can then reset the story with a new DNA batch (a different villain, or the Don's equally awful twin).

## Social

- **Visit friends' farms**, help water their vats.
- **Gift a clone:** send a friend one of your clones and they choose what happens to him.
- **Co-op orders:** big community orders ("the whole city wants him launched into the sun") that everyone contributes to.

## Monetisation

- **Energy = Fertiliser.** Tending and planting use fertiliser. It refills over time or can be bought.
- **Speed-ups:** **"Growth Serum"** finishes a vat instantly.
- **Premium equipment:** limited-time execution devices with special mini-games (a seasonal Christmas chimney, a Halloween pumpkin carver).
- **Decorations:** gnomes, flower beds, a tasteful memorial wall with every clone's photo.
- **Season pass:** **"Harvest Pass"**, with equipment skins, clone costumes and decorations.
- **Rewarded ads:** the ad plays on a TV in the clone's waiting room. He watches it with you, visibly bored.

## Art direction

- **Sunny storybook farm** by default: bright greens, soft shadows, cute buildings, butterflies.
- **Executions go full splatter:** saturated colour, comic-style gore, freeze frames, a screen-filling "DELIVERED" stamp.
- The clash between the two styles is the joke and the brand.
- The Don is a big, expressive cartoon character with a huge range of reactions. He's the mascot. Every clone wears his face.

## Audio

- A calm, folksy farm soundtrack with a mandolin and the occasional opera sample.
- Executions cut to a loud, orchestral or synth stinger, then back to the birds.
- The Don has a large set of voiced grunts, insults and pleas.

## Tone and ratings

- 17+ / PEGI 18. Stylised comic gore, no realistic violence, no torture framed as cruelty. Every killing is quick slapstick.
- The Don is fictional and a clear villain; the game never punches down.
- A **"Pie mode"** swaps every death for a pie in the face, for a lower rating or squeamish players, without changing the mechanics.

## Open questions

- Does the core joke keep working after hundreds of executions? The escalating clone story is the main answer; it needs a long-term content plan.
- How many mini-games is enough? Too few gets repetitive; too many is expensive. Probably 8 at launch, 1-2 per season.
- Does "repeatedly killing the same man" read as funny or disturbing to a mass audience? Needs early player testing with the cozy art direction.
- Is the farm view free-form (decorate anywhere) or a fixed layout? Free-form is more cozy but much more work.
