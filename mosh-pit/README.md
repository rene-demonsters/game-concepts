# Mosh Pit (working title)

A match-3 puzzle game set in a sweaty punk show. The tiles are punks in a packed crowd. Matching them starts circle pits, stage dives and full-blown riots that throw bodies off the stage.

It plays exactly like the cozy match-3 games people play for years: short levels, a map of hundreds of stages, boosters, lives and daily rewards. It just looks and sounds like a basement gig flyer that caught fire.

**Player promise:** the familiar, relaxing swap-and-cascade loop, with every combo turning into loud, joyful cartoon chaos.

Title availability has not been checked.

## Pillars

- **Cozy underneath.** No timers by default, no punishing difficulty spikes, levels of 2-4 minutes. A player who knows the genre should be fluent within ten seconds.
- **Punk on top.** Everything the player sees and hears belongs to the scene: zines, gig posters, gaffer tape, feedback, broken strings. The genre's sugary candy look is the thing we're rebelling against.
- **Cartoon violence, not cruelty.** Punks fly, bounce, crowd-surf and get flung into the bar, then get back up. Nobody gets hurt for real; everybody is having the time of their life. The pit is a celebration.
- **The store is part of the joke.** Lives, boosters and offers are in-world items with their own gags, so spending money feels like part of the bit, not a toll booth.

## Why match-3 fits a mosh pit

A match-3 board is already a crowd of bodies jostling into place. Swapping two neighbours is a shove. A cascade is a pit spreading through a crowd. The mechanics barely change; only the meaning does. That's the pitch: **the same game, filmed at a different venue.**

## Core mechanics

### Tiles

Each tile is a punk wearing a band shirt. Shirt colour and logo are the match type (5-6 types per level). Each fictional band has a distinct silhouette on its logo so the game stays readable for colour-blind players.

### Matches and specials

| Match | Special created | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 3 in a row | none | The three get flung out of the crowd |
| 4 in a row | **Stage Diver** | Surfs across the crowd, clearing a row or column |
| 2×2 square | **Crowd Surfer** | Gets passed hand-to-hand to a target tile and takes it out |
| L or T shape | **Wall of Death** | The crowd splits and slams back together, clearing a 3×3 area |
| 5 in a row | **Frontman** | Screams into the mic; every punk wearing the matched shirt jumps off-stage |

Combining two specials starts a bigger event. Two Stage Divers make a cross. A Frontman plus any special turns every punk of a type into that special. Two Frontmen trigger a **Riot**, which clears the board while the venue lights strobe.

### Obstacles (blockers)

- **Security guards:** take two hits to clear, and only from adjacent matches.
- **Barricades:** block movement, and get knocked over by nearby matches.
- **Spilled beer:** sticky tiles that can't be swapped until a match next to them clears the puddle.
- **Merch tables:** big 2×2 blockers that drop collectibles when they break.
- **Amps:** blow up after three nearby matches, clearing a cross and sometimes setting off a chain reaction.
- **Sleeping drunk:** sits in a cell and moves one step down every turn.

### Level goals

- Clear X punks of a given band.
- Break every security guard.
- **Crowd-surf the singer:** move a special "frontperson" tile from the top of the board to the stage edge at the bottom (the genre's "drop the ingredient" goal).
- Smash all the amps before the encore (move limit).

## Progression

The map is a **tour**. Each world is a city on the tour, and each venue is a set of 15-20 levels. Venues escalate: basement, squat, dive bar, skate park, festival tent, arena, and eventually a mansion party the band crashed by accident.

Clearing a venue unlocks a gig poster in the player's **flyer wall**, the collection meta. Rare event posters are limited-time collectibles.

A light band layer sits on top. The player's band (four characters) gets cosmetic upgrades as the tour goes on: new jackets, haircuts, a better van. No stat progression, so level difficulty stays clean and tunable.

## Monetisation

The standard cozy free-to-play model, played straight but themed.

- **Lives = teeth.** You start with five teeth and lose one for each failed level. They grow back over time (about 30 minutes each). Out of teeth? Buy **dentures** for a full refill, or wait.
- **Asking friends for lives:** friends mail you one of their teeth, in an envelope, with a note.
- **Continue screen:** "Out of moves? Bribe the sound engineer for 5 more songs" (+5 moves for coins).
- **Pre-level boosters:** Chain Wallet (lashes out to clear a row), Bouncer (removes any one tile), Cherry Bomb (dropped into the busiest area of the board, no aiming).
- **Unlimited lives timers** sold as **"All-Access Wristband: 2 hours"**.
- **Season pass:** **"Tour Pass"**, with free and premium tracks of cosmetics, posters and boosters.
- **Rewarded ads:** framed as a fake in-world commercial break between sets. The real ad plays inside a cracked CRT on the bar.

## Art direction

- **Photocopied zine collage:** halftone photos, torn tape, hand-cut lettering, safety pins, fluorescent spot colours (hot pink, acid green, safety orange) on black and newsprint.
- Punks are chunky, rounded and expressive, with big heads, so they still feel friendly in a puzzle grid. Think cartoon punk, not grimy realism.
- Each venue has its own colour scheme so the tour feels like it's going places.
- UI elements are stickers, stamps and gaffer tape. Level-complete screens are stamped like a hand at the door.

## Audio

- Every match is a short burst: a drum hit, a chord stab, a crowd yell. Cascades build into a full riff the longer they go, so a big chain literally becomes a song.
- Each venue has a loopable punk track at a volume that won't wear you out over a 30-minute session. Music should energise, not exhaust.
- Big moments (Riot, Frontman) get a guitar feedback swell and a crowd roar.

## Tone and ratings

- Aim for a 12+/PEGI 12 rating: slapstick, no blood, no real injuries, no drugs. Drinks are vague "cans".
- Punks always pop back up somewhere: in the bar, on a balcony, hanging from a lighting rig. That keeps it funny rather than mean.
- All bands, logos and venues are fictional.

## Open questions

- Does the venue-and-tour fiction need a story layer (band drama, rival bands), or is it better as pure vibe?
- How loud can the audio be before it breaks the "cozy" feel of a 30-minute evening session? Needs testing.
- Is the zine-collage look readable at phone size with 8×9 boards? Needs an early art test.
- Could an optional **"encore mode"** (timed, score attack) attract a more competitive crowd without hurting the cozy core?
