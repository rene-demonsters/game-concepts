# Scraphead (working title)

An open-world merge game set in a nuclear junkyard. You drag junk together to merge it into ever more ridiculous weapons and vehicles, burn back the radioactive Glow to claim land, and raise a crew of mutant critters who scavenge, harvest and build for you. Bit by bit, a toxic wasteland turns into the loudest raider camp on the map.

It plays like the cozy open-map merge games people sink months into: a free-form board you pan around, merge-3 rules, creatures that work while you watch, short challenge levels and a home camp that never ends. Every chain, though, ends in something with spikes, chains and a flamethrower.

**Player promise:** the relaxing pile-it-up-and-merge loop, a crew of horrible little helpers doing the chores, and the slow pleasure of turning a glowing dump into your own kingdom.

Title availability has not been checked.

## Pillars

- **Cozy underneath.** No fail state in the camp, no timers on the board, play in short or long sessions. Watching your mutants potter around should be half the fun.
- **Escalation is the joke.** Every chain starts with something pathetic (a bent nail, a soggy sock) and ends in something absurd. Players keep merging just to see what comes next.
- **Every merge is a small explosion.** Merges go off with sparks, smoke and a power chord. Merging on Glow burns it clean, so expanding your land literally means setting things on fire.
- **Lovable monsters.** The mutants and raiders look terrifying and act sweet. They have pet rats, write thank-you notes and wave at you.
- **The store is part of the joke.** Energy, gems and offers are in-world items with their own gags.

## Core mechanics

### The map

A large, free-form junkyard you pan and zoom around, not a fixed grid. Most of it is covered in **Glow**: bubbling green radioactive sludge. Items can sit on Glow, but nothing grows there and mutants won't walk on it.

### Merging

- Drag **3 identical items** together to merge them into one item of the next tier.
- Merge **5 at once** and you get **2** items of the next tier, so patience pays.
- Each merge burns away the Glow under it, opening a patch of clean ground. Clearing the map is a side effect of playing.
- Merges cascade: if a merge creates a third matching item next to two others, they merge too.

### Junk piles

Scrap heaps, wrecked cars, dumpsters and fridges are scattered across the map. Tapping or sending a mutant to one **harvests** it, producing base items. Bigger piles give better items but take longer and cost more Rage to harvest by hand.

### Mutants (your helpers)

Mutant critters hatch from cracked **radioactive barrels** found in the junk. Once out, they wander the clean land on their own and:

- **scavenge** junk piles and bring back items,
- **haul** items around and drop them near matches,
- **build** camp structures,
- **nap** in the sun when there's nothing to do.

Mutants also merge (3 of a kind make a bigger one). Bigger mutants work faster, carry more and can take on bigger piles. They have their own chains:

- Rat → Angry Rat → Two-Headed Rat → Rat in a Helmet → Rat King → Rat King on a Skateboard → Rat Tank
- Roach → Big Roach → Roach with Shades → Roach Biker → Roach Gang Leader
- Mangy Dog → Three-Eyed Dog → Spiked Hound → Hellhound with a Sidecar

Mutants need clean land to move on and somewhere to sleep, so burning back Glow and building shacks lets your crew grow.

### Item chains

Each chain is roughly 8-10 tiers long. The tiers near the top are rare showcase items.

- **Blades:** Bent Nail → Rusty Nail → Shiv → Kitchen Knife → Machete → Bumper Sword → Chainsaw → Chainsaw on a Stick → Chainsaw Motorbike → Chainsaw Motorbike with Flamethrower
- **Armour:** Soggy Sock → Rag → Leather Strip → Spiked Collar → Studded Jacket → Road-Sign Shoulder Pads → Hubcap Armour → Walking Junk Fortress
- **Fuel:** Oil Drop → Can of Oil → Jerry Can → Molotov → Barrel of Boom → Nuclear Barbecue Grill
- **Rage Cola:** Flat Can → Fizzy Can → Six-Pack → Cola Cooler → Rage Fountain. Tapping a Rage Cola item turns it into Rage (energy).
- **Scrap Stars:** Bottle Cap → Crushed Cap → Star Badge → Sheriff Star → Golden Hubcap. Spent on camp buildings.

### Threats

Not everything in the junkyard is friendly, but nothing fights back in real time.

- **Sludge Blobs** sit on Glow and keep it from being burned. Merging a fuel item next to one makes it pop.
- **Chrome Choir sentry bots** guard locked areas. Each one names the item it wants smashed into it (for example a tier-5 blade) and stands down when you deliver it.
- **Toxic crates** look like loot, but merging next to them releases a small Glow cloud.

## Modes

### The Camp (endless sandbox)

The home map. There's no goal and no end. You build the raider camp, grow your mutant crew, collect every chain and decorate. Camp landmarks give the space personality:

- a tyre fire that grows into a **towering bonfire sculpture**,
- a **bone throne** for the gang boss,
- a **drive-in cinema** that plays bootleg action movies,
- a **mutant petting zoo**,
- a **Thunder Pit** arena where the gang holds races and duels.

### Raids (challenge levels)

Short, hand-built levels on their own maps, reached from a world map of wasteland locations. Each has a goal:

- Build a Chainsaw Motorbike.
- Burn all the Glow off the map.
- Free a caged raider from a Chrome Choir compound.
- Collect 30 Scrap Stars.

Raids give 1-3 stars for speed and side goals, and the rewards come back to camp: rare items, barrels holding new mutants and new chains. Hard raids appear every few levels as **Big Raids** with a short story beat.

## Story and cast

A light, funny story told through raid briefings and camp conversations.

- **Big Mama Gristle:** gang boss, fierce, deeply maternal, calls everyone "sweetpea".
- **Sprocket:** a teenage mutant mechanic who idolises the player.
- **Deacon Dread:** a gloomy preacher who blesses every weapon you make.
- **Pickles:** a rat the size of a dog. Not a raider. Thinks he is.
- **The Chrome Choir:** a rival gang of chrome-plated robots who sing in four-part harmony and want the junkyard turned into a parking lot.

The player is a newcomer: a scavenger with a knack for putting junk together, and the only one who can make the mutants listen.

## Monetisation

The standard open-map merge free-to-play model, played straight but themed.

- **Energy = Rage.** Harvesting big junk piles by hand and starting raids cost Rage. It refills slowly, comes from merging Rage Cola chains, and can be bought.
- **Gems = nuts** (as in nuts and bolts). Earned slowly, bought in the store. Used to speed up mutant work, crack barrels instantly, buy extra storage and pop big Glow areas.
- **Loot crates:** themed bundles with rare mutants and high-tier items. Contents are shown before purchase, no blind boxes.
- **Raid continues:** "Out of time? Bribe the lookout for 2 more minutes."
- **Event pass:** **"Raid Pass"**, with free and premium tracks of camp decorations, mutants and boosters.
- **Time-limited events:** separate event maps (a Halloween graveyard, a beach raid, the Chrome Choir showdown) with their own chains and exclusive camp items.
- **Daily free reward:** punch the camp's punching bag once a day.

## Art direction

- **Post-apocalyptic Saturday-morning cartoon:** chunky, bold outlines, bright saturated colours against a dusty orange sky. Mad, not miserable.
- **Glow** is vivid acid green, bubbly and almost cute, so burning it back feels like weeding a garden rather than fighting a disaster.
- Items get more ornate and **more glowy** as they go up a chain, so a high-tier item reads as precious at a glance.
- Mutants and raiders are huge, scarred, mutated and adorable. Big expressive faces, little details (a teddy bear on a belt, painted nails).
- Merges get a little explosion and a small "showcase" moment for high tiers.

## Audio

- Lo-fi garage rock and sludgy riffs at a relaxing tempo. The camp should sound like a hangout, not a battle.
- Every merge has a satisfying crunch or clank that gets bigger with tier. Burning Glow makes a soft sizzle.
- Mutants have little voice barks (squeaks, grunts, snores). Raiders have catchphrases.

## Tone and ratings

- Aim for 12+/PEGI 12: weapons are props, nobody gets hurt on screen. Violence is implied and off-screen ("the parade went great, three cars exploded").
- No real drugs or alcohol. "Rage Cola" is the joke; it's soda.
- The wasteland is fictional; no real-world disasters or places.

## Open questions

- How big should the camp map be, and how much Glow should one session clear? This decides the whole pacing.
- Should mutants also work while the game is closed (idle progress), or only while you watch?
- How many raids between camp chapters? Raids are the hand-built content and the hardest to scale.
- Is the free-form map readable on a phone screen once it gets crowded? Needs an early prototype of zoom, drag and auto-sort.
- Should we keep raider orders (the merge-2 version's request cards) as a light quest layer in the camp?
