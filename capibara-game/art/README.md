# Concept art directions

Four base directions and a textured cutout variation explore the same lighthouse landing, featuring the unnamed female capybara captain and shrimp first mate. The shared scene, outfits, and palette make the differences in rendering easier to compare. These are illustrations for choosing an art direction; character proportions, costume details, and staging remain provisional.

Generated with the built-in image generation tool. The exact [generation prompts](prompts.md) are saved for reuse.

**Preferred direction: flat cutout animation (04).** The flat version was preferred after comparing it with the textured variation. Use it as the reference for further character and animation development.

## 1. Comic book

Based on the repository's [comic-book style guide](../../art-styles/comic-book.md), adapted to a peaceful children's mystery. Bold ink contours, deep plum shadows, crisp cel shading, paper grain, and selective halftone. Strong contrast helps characters and objects stand out.

![Comic-book concept: capybara captain and shrimp at the lighthouse](01-comic-book.png)

## 2. Soft colored clay

A handmade miniature world in muted teal, peach, ochre, terracotta, and lavender. Rounded forms, matte surfaces, subtle thumbprints, and soft light give the environment a tactile quality. A game could use still renders or generated illustrations with this finish while keeping animation modest.

![Soft-clay concept: capybara captain and shrimp at the lighthouse](02-soft-clay.png)

## 3. Gouache storybook

Hand-painted gouache with layered cut-paper shapes, dry-brush texture, and warm light. Broad shapes keep the scene readable while material texture gives it character. This direction suits illustrated locations, separate character poses, and small environmental effects.

![Gouache storybook concept: capybara captain and shrimp at the lighthouse](03-gouache-storybook.png)

## 4. Flat cutout animation

An animation-conscious direction with rounded geometric shapes, flat colors, sparse outline accents, and clearly separated limbs. Teal, coral, ochre, and deep blue preserve warmth and character. Deliberate silhouettes, expressions, and color choices provide the finish with little surface detail to maintain between poses.

![Flat cutout concept: capybara captain and shrimp at the lighthouse](04-flat-cutout.png)

### How it would animate

Build each character as a small collection of overlapping parts: head, body, arm sections, hands, and feet, with separate facial features. The shrimp uses a curved main body, tail fan, simple legs, eyes, and short antennae. Sleeves and body overlaps hide the pivots. Keep the main camera angle consistent and use a few alternate hand or body drawings for actions that rotations cannot describe convincingly.

An initial animation set could include:

- **Idle:** an occasional blink, slight head tilt, and subtle body motion. The shrimp can twitch his antennae or settle his tail.
- **Talking:** swap between a small set of mouth shapes and change eyebrows or eyes. Exact lip synchronization is optional.
- **Walking:** a reusable cycle of leg and arm poses, a small body bob, and movement across the scene. Give the shrimp a short scuttling cycle.
- **Pointing and inspecting:** reuse arm rotations and a few replacement hand poses; lean the body or turn the head toward the target.
- **Cooperation:** stage a small set of deliberate poses, such as the captain holding a panel while the shrimp disappears through a slot and reappears with an object.
- **Environmental response:** gently swing a lantern, lift a door, or turn a wheel using separate prop layers.

Production art would be drawn and exported as separate transparent parts, including enough overlap to cover joints and hidden surfaces. This concept illustration is a flattened style reference. The first animation prototype should test one idle, one walk, and one pointing interaction at actual gameplay size before producing the full cast.

This is the preferred direction for further development. Preserve its clean surfaces, readable shapes, and simple character parts.

## 4b. Textured cutout variation

Experiment retained for comparison. The flat version (04) was preferred.

A variation of the flat cutout illustration with subtle paper grain, dry-brush pigment variation, and restrained surface detail. The background carries more texture, while character faces, outlines, hands, and joints stay clean. The aim is a warmer handmade finish with the same simple shapes and animation approach.

![Textured cutout concept: capybara captain and shrimp at the lighthouse](05-textured-cutout.png)

For production, bake the texture into each separate character part so it moves with that part. Keep its scale and contrast consistent across replacement mouth, hand, and body drawings. Use enough painted overlap around joints to prevent gaps. Background texture can be richer because it does not need to change with each pose.

This remains a flattened concept illustration. Creating the layered production assets and a short animation test would establish how well the finish holds up in motion. The exact [edit prompt](05-textured-cutout-prompt.md) is included.

## Shared production considerations

- Keep the shrimp readable at gameplay scale; his relative size needs a consistent rule across scenes.
- Separate characters, foreground props, and backgrounds when producing actual game assets.
- Give interactive objects clear outlines and space around them.
- Keep relevant color cues paired with symbols, shapes, or spoken descriptions.
- The final game needs distinct closed-door and open-door states; these illustrations explore the overall scene rather than specifying every puzzle state.
