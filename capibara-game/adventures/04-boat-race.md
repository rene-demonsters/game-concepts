# The Great Backwards Boat Race

Story and puzzle treatment. All character and place names remain undecided.

## Story

The annual harbor race is about to begin, but the rehearsal is a wonderful mess. One boat turns in circles, another leans sideways, and a boastful rival has built such an elaborate sail that it catches the starting arch. The shrimp suspects sabotage. The capybara suggests beginning with the loose rudder lying on the dock.

The race organizer asks the duo to help everyone prepare. They discover several ordinary problems: steering parts were assembled incorrectly, cargo was loaded on one side, and sail sections were mixed up at the repair stall. Meanwhile, a current has carried two course markers into a sheltered inlet.

The organizer then realizes that the prize is missing. The harbor courier delivered its crate to the race club on the wrong island after rain blurred the destination picture. Clues on the delivery receipt reveal where it went. The friendly rival offers a useful spare mast fitting once the duo helps shorten the oversized sail.

With the boats repaired, the crew sails out to recover the markers and collect the prize. Back at the start, a tiny missing pin prevents the flag mechanism from working. The shrimp locates it beneath the judging platform and finally gets a task whose small scale suits him perfectly.

The race is a short celebratory ending. The child chooses a few picture-marked routes with unlimited opportunities to correct course. Every crew finishes and receives recognition; the capybara and shrimp receive a special commendation for getting the event afloat. The rival proudly announces that the boat now travels forwards almost all the time.

## Locations and progression

Starting dock and repair stall → three neighboring moorings → sheltered inlet and neighboring island landing → starting platform → race finale. The three boat repairs can be completed in any order.

## Puzzle chain

| Step | Goal and clue | Player action | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Fix the steering | A simple assembly card shows the tiller, rudder, and matching connection shapes. The current arrangement leaves one socket empty. | Fit the parts into the matching sockets and test the wheel on the moored boat. | The first boat turns correctly; its owner lends a floating retrieval hoop. |
| 2. Balance the cargo | The boat visibly lists. A large balance gauge uses a centered boat picture instead of numbers. | Move crates between marked left and right deck spaces until the picture is level. | The second crew lends a long-handled hook for the course markers. |
| 3. Correct the rival's sail | The sail plan shows a clear silhouette. Several cloth sections extend far beyond it. | Select the matching sections from reusable pieces at the repair stall, then hand them to the sailmaker. | The rival gets a manageable sail and gives the duo a spare mast fitting needed for their own boat. |
| 4. Read the wind | A broken fitting prevents the duo from raising their sail. The dock's windsock and travel chart show a safe departure heading. | Install the fitting and choose the chart arrow that matches the windsock. | Depart for the inlet. Incorrect choices prompt an explanation while the boat remains moored. |
| 5. Recover the markers | Two buoys are caught among floating reeds. Their symbols match empty positions on the course chart. | Use the hoop to keep each buoy alongside, then the hook to free its rope. Return it to its matching chart position. | Restore the race route. These are select-and-use interactions with no timed aiming. |
| 6. Locate the prize | The courier receipt retains a roof symbol and part of an island outline. The travel map shows three possible landings. | Compare the visual clues, visit the matching club, and exchange the organizer's claim token for the crate. | Recover the prize. The club attendant helps load it. |
| 7. Repair the starting flag | The handle moves, but a joint is missing its retaining pin. A small glint is visible below the platform. | Let the shrimp crawl beneath it and retrieve the pin. Fit it while the captain holds the joint aligned. | The organizer raises the flag and offers to begin the finale. |

## Replay variations

- Cargo layouts start in different unbalanced arrangements, each with a verified solution.
- The rival needs one of two sail silhouettes, with matching plan and available pieces.
- The mistaken delivery destination varies with its receipt clues; all possible club landings are accessible after the boat repairs.

## Hints and completion rules

The organizer records repaired boats and unfinished tasks in a picture checklist. Each borrowed tool is shown in a short demonstration before entering the shared inventory.

The race waits for the player. Repaired boats remain repaired, cargo moves are reversible, and sail pieces are never cut or discarded by the player. Essential tools cannot be handed away elsewhere. Returning markers opens routes rather than closing them. The starting pin can be inspected early but is never required for sailing. The finale has no failure state, rank requirement, or speed-dependent reward.
