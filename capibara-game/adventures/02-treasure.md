# The Treasure Nobody Wanted

Story and puzzle treatment. All character and place names remain undecided.

## Story

After a windy night, a wooden chest washes onto the harbor beach. Inside are a bent spoon, a cracked bell, an enormous button, and a map with several empty picture spaces. The shrimp appoints himself guardian of the treasure. On discovering that none of it is gold, he decides someone has packed the wrong chest.

The harbor archivist recognizes the chest as belonging to a retired captain who once lived on a nearby island. Its contents look familiar to different residents. A baker remembers using a very long spoon at a communal feast. A ferry operator recognizes the bell's carved emblem. A sailmaker remembers the button from a coat used to wrap a rescued passenger.

Each object leads to a small problem and a remembered story. The captain helped people on her travels and kept these objects as reminders. As the duo restores and returns the keepsakes, their owners provide picture tiles that fit the map. The apparently useless collection becomes a record of friendships across the islands.

The completed map leads to the retired captain's old boathouse. Inside is a memory cabinet with an empty central frame and an unfinished invitation to a reunion. A storm carried off the chest before the invitation could be delivered.

The duo finishes the invitation using the people and places they have discovered. At the reunion, the retired captain thanks them and gives the shrimp an official job: opening the cabinet's very small latch. He proudly declares the treasure priceless, while checking whether there might also be a biscuit at the party.

## Locations and progression

Harbor beach and archive → bakery, ferry landing, and sail loft in any order → retired captain's island boathouse → reunion. The three keepsake branches are independent.

## Puzzle chain

| Step | Goal and clue | Player action | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Open the chest | Sand is packed into a small latch cover. The shrimp sees the catch through a gap. | Rinse the cover with a refillable beach pail; let the shrimp reach the catch while the captain lifts the lid. | Obtain the three keepsakes and incomplete map. |
| 2. Find the owners | Each object bears a clear emblem also displayed at a local business. The archivist explains that the symbols are signatures. | Match the spoon, bell, and button to the bakery, ferry, and sail loft. | Open three optional-order conversations and repair tasks. |
| 3. Restore the spoon | The baker's photograph shows the spoon's original curved handle. A shaping jig has three large pegs. | Place the spoon in the jig and select the peg arrangement that matches the photograph. | The baker uses it to stir a festival pot and contributes a picture tile showing the old captain's first visit. |
| 4. Restore the bell | The ferry operator has a padded repair cradle and a reusable mending clamp. The sound is dull because the clapper is missing. | Fit the bell into the cradle and close the clamp. The shrimp enters a retired model ferry on the shelf to retrieve its stored clapper; attach it with the provided cord. | Hear the restored bell and receive a tile depicting a foggy crossing. |
| 5. Return the button | The sailmaker's spare coats have different button shapes. The matching coat is missing its largest button. | Compare the button with the coat outlines and hand over the right coat and button. The sailmaker completes the sewing. | Reveal a photograph tucked inside the lining and receive the third map tile. |
| 6. Complete the route | Tile edges match the map's empty spaces. Buildings and coastline continue across their boundaries. | Fit and rotate the three large tiles. | Reveal a sheltered route to the boathouse. The ordinary travel map handles the voyage. |
| 7. Prepare the reunion | The boathouse has an invitation board with three empty emblem spaces. | Place the bakery, ferry, and sailmaker emblems. Ask the harbor courier to carry the completed invitations. | The guests arrive; the shrimp opens the cabinet latch for the celebration. |

## Replay variations

- The map tiles depict different coastlines and fit different matching slots, while always leading to the same boathouse.
- The spoon's reference photograph selects one of two clearly distinguishable handle curves.
- The matching coat and the model ferry can change shelf positions within their already accessible rooms.

## Hints and completion rules

The map does not require geographic knowledge or reading. Continuous coastline shapes provide the solution, with spoken descriptions available. The emblem board recalls people already met rather than testing memory without support.

The captain keeps the map throughout. Returning a keepsake permanently records its story and adds its tile to the shared inventory. No branch requires another branch's reward. The model ferry opens on inspection and cannot trap the shrimp. Repair tools stay available until their task is complete. Invitations are delivered through a short scene, with no real-time wait or obligation to revisit every location.
